# synthMachine carrier board

A 100 x 100 mm carrier for the Daisy Seed (or Seed3) that drives the synth: the
19-button key matrix, 5 pots, USB-C for MIDI and power, a MAX98306 class-D amp
for the two enclosed Adafruit speakers, and a TPA6138A2 headphone amp with a
switched jack that mutes the speakers when headphones are plugged in.

Buttons and pots stay on the panel and connect with JST-XH leads, so the board
can be mounted anywhere in the enclosure and every panel part unplugs for
service. USB-C, the headphone jack and the Seed's own USB port all sit on the
top board edge, so one enclosure wall carries all three.

Everything here is generated by `gen_kicad.py` so the schematic and the PCB
always agree with each other and with the pin map in `../synthMachine.cpp`.

![PCB render](pcb_render.png)

## Open it

Open `synth_machine.kicad_pro` in KiCad 9 or 10. ERC is clean (a fresh KiCad 10
install reports "library not in configuration" warnings until it has created
its default library tables on first launch). The PCB is placed and routed with
GND pours on both sides. Remaining DRC items are hole-clearance nits inside
KiCad's own USB-C footprint and a few thermal-spoke notes; both can be ignored.

## Regenerate and route

```bash
KICAD_PY=/Applications/KiCad/KiCad.app/Contents/Frameworks/Python.framework/Versions/Current/bin/python3
$KICAD_PY hardware/gen_kicad.py                                   # schematic + placed PCB
$KICAD_PY hardware/route.py --java <java25>/bin/java --jar freerouting-2.4.1.jar
$KICAD_PY hardware/route.py --pours-only                          # rebuild just the GND pours
sh hardware/fab.sh                                                # Gerbers, BOM, CPL, test print
```

`gen_kicad.py` overwrites the schematic and PCB, so re-run `route.py` after it.
`route.py` lays down the hand-routed nets in `PREROUTES` (audio paths and the
MAX98306 fan-out) locked, exports a Specctra DSN, runs Freerouting headless
(needs Java 25; a portable JRE from adoptium.net works), imports the result, then
re-adds and fills the pours. Net classes: 0.2 mm default with 0.15 mm clearance,
0.6 mm "Power" for +5V and the filtered speaker lines, 0.4 mm for VBUS, 0.3 mm
for the amp-to-bead speaker lines.

| Key headers | K1..K19, JST-XH 2-pin, 10 down the left edge and 9 along the bottom | One lead per button. Pin 1 = matrix row (or D10 for C4), pin 2 = diode (or GND for C4). Silkscreen names each header. |
| Matrix diodes | D1..D18, 1N4148W SOD-123 | Next to each header. Columns D4..D9 driven low, rows D1..D3 with pull-ups, exactly as `scanButtonMatrix()`. |

## Wiring the panel

- **Buttons:** each button gets a 2-pin XH lead with 2.8 mm (0.110") female
  disconnects on the button end. Polarity does not matter. Pre-crimped XH leads
  (Amazon kits, 20 to 30 cm) are the easy route; solder the disconnects on.
- **Pots:** 3-pin XH lead per pot: pin 1 to one outer lug, pin 2 to the wiper,
  pin 3 to the other outer lug. Swap the outer lugs if a pot turns backwards.
- **Speakers:** plug straight into J5/J6. Keep both plugged the same way so the
  speakers stay in phase.
- **Board position:** anywhere. The only constraint is the top edge, which
  needs cutouts for USB-C, the headphone jack and the Seed's USB.

## Why the headphones have their own amp

The MAX98306 outputs are bridge-tied class-D with no ground reference: no series
caps on the speakers, never join the two channels' minus legs, and no headphones
on them (their sleeve is a shared ground). So the headphone jack gets its own
TPA6138A2 driven from the same Seed line out, and the jack's switch contact
mutes the speaker amp. Plug in, speakers go quiet; unplug, they return.

## Ordering (JLCPCB)

`sh hardware/fab.sh` writes everything into `hardware/fab/`:

- `synth_machine_gerbers.zip`: upload unchanged for the PCB. 2 layers, 100 x
  100 mm, FR-4 1.6 mm, 1 oz, HASL lead-free or ENIG, "remove order number".
- `jlcpcb_bom.csv` and `jlcpcb_cpl.csv`: for SMT assembly, top side, economic.
  Every SMD part carries an LCSC number: the two amps (extended parts), the
  matrix diodes, the ferrite beads, Q1 and all 0805 passives. The CPL
  pre-rotates TSSOP/TDFN parts by -90 degrees for JLC's convention; in the
  placement preview check that U2's and U3's pin-1 dots sit on the silkscreen
  triangles, and that the 18 diodes all show the cathode band on the pin-1 end.
- `synth_machine_1to1_11x17.pdf`: 1:1 print, mostly useful now to check the
  enclosure cutouts on the top edge.

Hand-soldered after assembly: Seed sockets, 19 + 5 XH headers, 2 PH sockets,
the jack, the USB-C receptacle, the fuse and C1. `digikey_tht_parts.csv` is a
Digi-Key list for those plus the XH housings/contacts, pots and speakers.

## Before ordering

1. Seed pin-1 corner on `DaisySeed_2x20` against a real Seed.
2. USB-C and jack positions against your enclosure wall: the USB-C shell
   overhangs the edge by about 1 mm, the jack nose is flush.
3. USB power budget: two 4 ohm speakers at full tilt exceed a USB-A port's
   500 mA. Use a USB-C supply or a 1.5 A-capable port.

## Firmware

`../synthMachine.cpp` builds for this board with `make HW=carrier` (a plain
`make` targets the breadboard prototype). The carrier profile enables
`EXTERNAL` USB MIDI, drives the speaker mute on D11 with the rules below, reads
HP_DET on D13 and holds HP_MUTE (D14) low until a plug is detected.

Still open: BTN1..6 (PCB nodes above; the prototype had them on different
nodes) have no function assigned yet. They are the `-1` entries in
`NOTE_MAPPING`.

### MUTE drive rules (this revision)

MUTE (D11) and HP_DET meet at Q1's base through R6 and R5 (10k each), and
HP_DET's pull-up R3 is 100k. So:

- driving MUTE high mutes the speakers regardless of the jack;
- leaving MUTE as an input lets the jack decide;
- driving MUTE **low** holds Q1 off and keeps the speakers on even with
  headphones in, because R6 to ground beats R3. Firmware never drives it low;
  "unmute" is tri-state.

While nothing is plugged in, the jack's switch ties HP_DET to the headphone
amp's left output, so audio peaks above about 0.65 V would turn Q1 on and gate
the speaker amp. Firmware keeps the headphone amp muted (HP_MUTE low) until a
plug is detected, which holds that node near ground.

The speakers default to on at reset, so firmware can only race the codec's
start-up pop (it wins, since GPIO is set before the Seed init), and DFU mode or
a crash leaves them live.

### Next revision

Give MUTE its own transistor (second MMBT3904, 10k base resistor, collector on
AMP_SD) and a 10k pull-up from MUTE to +3V3. The two mute sources then become
independent open-collector pull-downs, firmware can use plain push-pull drive,
and the speakers default to muted at reset, in DFU mode and after a crash.
